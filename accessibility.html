Accessibility Statement

Last updated: 20 October 2026. Applies to the Sunny desktop app, this website, and the Sunny project generally.

Sunny exists to help people who find computers difficult. It would be strange to build an accessible tool and then make the tool itself hard to use. We work to WCAG 2.1 Level AA across the app, the website, and everything we publish.

Our commitment

We want Sunny to be usable by as many people as possible, including people who rely on a screen reader, a keyboard, voice control, high contrast mode, or text magnification. Accessibility is not a feature we bolt on at the end. It shapes how we design the interface from the first sketch.

What Sunny supports

  • Text size and magnification. The whole interface scales up to 175% without clipping or losing function. Text resizing is a setting, not a browser trick.
  • High contrast mode. One tap switches to pure black backgrounds, gold accents, 3px borders, and enlarged type. Sunny also follows your system's prefers-contrast setting on its own.
  • Readable typeface. Atkinson Hyperlegible throughout. Every letterform is designed to be told apart at a glance, which matters for low vision.
  • Text to speech. Sunny can read every answer aloud, with a plain mute switch when you want silence.
  • Voice input. Press and hold the microphone and speak. You never need to type if you would rather not.
  • Large touch targets. Every button is at least 48 by 48 pixels, so it is easy to hit with a shaky hand or a large finger.
  • Full keyboard control. You can reach every control without a mouse, and the focus ring is always visible.
  • Screen reader labels. Buttons and controls announce what they do, and status changes are announced as they happen.
  • Reduced motion. Sunny follows your system's reduce-motion setting. No animation plays if you have asked for none.
  • Plain language. Answers are written for clarity, not jargon.

Conformance status

This site and the Sunny app are built toward WCAG 2.1 Level AA. We assess it against the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.1 and test with:

  • Keyboard-only navigation
  • Screen readers on Windows, macOS, iOS, and Android
  • Browser zoom to 200% and 400%
  • Windows high contrast mode
  • prefers-reduced-motion and prefers-contrast emulation
  • Automated contrast and structure checks on every build

Known limitations

We would rather name a gap than pretend it is not there. These are the ones we know about today:

  • Screen reader announcements in the live activity log are not always complete. Long answers are appended to the log, and some screen readers announce only the first line of an update.
  • The voice input model is small, so quiet or accented speech is transcribed less reliably than typed input. Typing always works, and nothing depends on voice alone.
  • Cooking mode in older builds did not scale the step text with the system text size setting. Fixed in the current release.

Feedback and reporting a barrier

If something gets in your way, please tell us. We read every report, and we treat a barrier as a bug rather than a suggestion.

  • Report a barrier: open an issue at github.com/MT25MB/Sunny/issues. Please say which screen you were on, what you were trying to do, and what happened instead.
  • Ask a question: open a discussion at github.com/MT25MB/Sunny/discussions.
  • Need an accommodation for a release, install, or setup session? Ask in an issue and we will do our best to help.

We aim to reply within five working days and to tell you what we plan to do about any barrier we confirm.

How we test

Accessibility checks run in CI on every push, so a contrast regression or a missing label fails the build rather than shipping. Manual testing with real assistive technology happens before each release.

What we are still working on

  • More screen reader coverage for the cooking and medication flows
  • A larger transcription model for quieter speech
  • Full translation of the interface into more languages
